She's been in the bakery since before she can remember. Her earliest memories include the smell of it — bread, warmth, flour — which means the smell of the bakery is not a smell she notices the way other people notice it. It's just how things smell.

She knows which bins to use. She knows the difference between doughs at different stages of proofing by the feel of them, which she worked out by being there when her mother was working and asking questions that her mother answered. She doesn't help with the technical parts. She helps with things she can help with: restacking trays, answering the door when it rings at six-thirty if her mother's hands are full.

She's nine. She goes to Ines's sessions on Tuesdays and Thursdays. On the other days she moves between the bakery and the courtyard and the homes of the Ferreira kids and wherever else she's supposed to be or not supposed to be but ends up anyway.

She has no particular opinion about inheriting the bakery. Nobody has asked her yet because she's nine. If they asked she'd say she wasn't sure, which is true. The bakery is her mother's thing. She might have her own thing when she knows what it is.

What she knows she likes: the early morning. She's been brought in early on Saturdays sometimes when her mother needs the company, and there's something about the city before it's decided anything that she finds she wants more of. She doesn't have the words for it yet. She just knows she doesn't want to be asleep for it.

Her mother knows this too. She watched her notice.
