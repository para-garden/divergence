Bea had drawn the shelf three times before Ines said anything about the wood.

The first drawing was just a rectangle. The second had dimensions — 80 cm, she'd decided, because that's how wide the alcove was. The third had the brackets marked in, little triangles at the corners, which she'd copied from the shelf in Ines's kitchen without being told to.

"What kind of timber?" Ines asked.

Bea didn't know there were kinds. Ines showed her the merchant's list on the screen — pine, oak, MDF, plywood, each with a price per metre. Oak was pretty. Oak was also eight euros a metre.

"How many metres do you need?"

Bea looked at her drawing. 80 cm. She wrote 0.8.

"And it needs to hold weight. So how thick?"

This took longer. They found a calculator and a table of load ratings. Bea wanted to put her ceramics on it — the ones she'd been making at Tomás's, three of them so far, more coming. She estimated. Ines didn't correct the estimate when it was probably low. Bea got to 18 mm, which meant a different price column.

She did the multiplication twice because the first answer seemed wrong. It wasn't wrong. Oak was still oak.

She sat with it.

"Pine's fine," she said eventually. "It'll look okay."

Ines handed her the pencil back. "Write the order down so you don't forget what you decided."

Bea wrote: *pine, 80cm, 18mm, brackets x2*. Then, underneath, without being asked: *oak later maybe*.
