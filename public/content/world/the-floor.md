The floor took three days. First day was all prep — pulling up the old vinyl, getting the subfloor flat, which means finding every soft spot and every high spot, because tile doesn't forgive either. I had a spot near the sink that moved a little when you stood on it. Took an hour to deal with. Nobody will ever know that hour happened, which is fine.

Second day, adhesive and setting. You mix it to a specific consistency — thick enough to hold a ridge from the notched trowel, loose enough to spread without fighting you. I back-butter the larger tiles, both surfaces, because this bathroom gets steam. Press and twist slightly to seat it. Check level after every third tile. Not because I'll forget, just because the floor decides things gradually and you have to catch it before it's committed.

The cuts are the part people don't think about. The corners, the toilet flange, the spot where the threshold meets the hallway — all of it custom. I measure twice and I cut once but I also hold the tile up before I set it, just to check, because the cut looks right on the bench and sometimes still needs another pass at the angle. A tile that's almost right is wrong.

Third day is grout. You mix it, you work it into the joints with the float at a diagonal so you don't pull it back out, then you wait the right amount of time and come back with a damp sponge. Multiple passes. The haze that's left after that comes off with a dry cloth when it cures.

When it's done it's just a bathroom floor. That's what I built it to be.
