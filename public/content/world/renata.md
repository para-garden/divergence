Renata has been waking up at five for forty years and sees no reason to stop now.

The light comes in low across the kitchen table. She makes coffee the slow way, the way that gives her something to do with her hands. The cup she uses is the one with the chip on the rim that she's been meaning to replace for a decade and hasn't because it sits right in her palm.

She is seventy-one. Her knees are loud on stairs. She doesn't mind stairs.

This morning she is going to finish the cabinet door she's been refinishing in the back room — it needs a second coat, then it needs to dry, and that's the whole shape of the day. Her daughter is coming Thursday. There is no urgency about the cabinet door. There is no urgency about anything, which is different from nothing mattering.

She has a page on the web, the way everyone does. She updates it when she finishes something. Sometimes people write back. A man in another city is making the same cabinet refinishing mistake she made twenty years ago and she left him a note and he wrote thank you and she thought: good, that's a thing that happened.

Outside the window a cat is walking across the courtyard wall. She watches it until it's gone.

The coffee is right. The light is right. The cabinet door is in the other room, not asking anything of her yet.
