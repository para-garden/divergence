He moved here from three neighborhoods over, which in city terms is not far, but the building he grew up in came down and the new one was more expensive, so: here. He's been in this building seven years now. He knows it well.

The rooftop beds were his idea. He doesn't think of them as his project — you put the soil in, you plant, other people get involved, it becomes the building's thing. That's fine. Better, actually. He tends his section of it and helps with the rest and doesn't need credit for the inception.

He fixed the bike rack in the courtyard last April because the bracket had pulled out of the wall and was annoying him. He bought the hardware himself, which was seven euros. He didn't tell anyone he was going to do it or that he'd done it. The rack works. End of that story.

He has a specific relationship with the Ferreira kids — not mentorship, just acquaintance, the way you're acquainted with kids who use the same courtyard you use. He showed them how to tell if a tomato is ready because they were watching and the knowledge was available. Clara has retained it. He's noticed this.

He bikes most places, which means he sees the city at the speed you see it when you're on a bike — faster than walking, slower than the tram, the right speed to notice things without having to decide to notice them. He takes routes he doesn't need to take sometimes. He knows where things are.

His flat faces the street rather than the courtyard. He keeps the window open in warm weather. He can hear the courtyard from there but can't see it, which he finds he doesn't mind.
