She saw him at the corner, both of them going somewhere, both stopping because that's what you do. They hadn't been in the same place since January.

Five minutes. Not a real conversation — a real conversation requires sitting down and not being on the way to something — but not empty either. He told her the thing about his situation, briefly, the version you tell when you have five minutes. She listened in the particular way of people who are actually listening and not waiting for their turn. She said something that was useful.

She mentioned a thing she was working on. He asked one question about it. The question was good — he always asks good questions, it's how he's wired — and she answered it and felt the answer clarify itself as she said it, which is a specific service that good questions provide.

They said they'd find time to actually talk. Maybe they would. Maybe they'd see each other again in six months at another corner. Both were acceptable outcomes. They like each other, they're just both in the middle of their lives.

She got to the thing she was going to. He went the other direction. The corner went back to being a corner.

She thought about his question later. The answer she'd given had been right. She hadn't known that until she said it.
