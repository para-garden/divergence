The kids come Tuesday and Thursday and every other Friday. We started two years ago with four of them — now there are seven, which is the most the table fits without someone sitting on the counter, which Luca does on purpose.

I don't plan very far ahead. We read things and talk about them. We do maths when there's a reason. Last month we were pricing timber for a shelf Bea wanted to build, which turned into three sessions on fractions, which nobody noticed until afterward. That felt right. If I had to explain what I'm doing I would say: I give them real things to think about and I don't pretend the questions have easy answers. Some of them push back hard. Good. I push back harder.

The flat is basically the table. I got it at a salvage place, it's too big for the room, I do not care at all. There are usually people at it in the evenings. Sometimes a parent will stay after pickup and we end up talking until ten. Daphne comes by with coffee and reads while I'm chopping something. She doesn't need to be talked at, which is good because I will talk, but she says things when it matters and I trust her more than I trust most people.

I cook for whoever's there. It doesn't require notice. The amount adjusts.

A few parents were nervous at first — no syllabus, no reports, nothing to measure. I get it. We're all carrying old ideas about what learning needs to look like. What I have to offer instead is: come watch them for an afternoon. They argue about real things. They know when they don't know something and they want to find out. Bea built the shelf. It holds weight.
