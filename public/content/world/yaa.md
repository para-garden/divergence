Yaa has an opinion about the cooling fans on the old Prusa printers and she isn't shy about sharing it. Rattly. "Like a bee stuck in a cup," she told Kwame when he asked, and he laughed, and now that's what everyone calls it: the bee-cup sound. She likes that it stuck.

She took three of her mother's groundnut cakes this morning. Two for her, one for Efua, who never eats enough before the bus. She doesn't feel guilty about this. Her mother knows. The cakes are for eating.

The thing she's working out right now — and she keeps working it out, like a loose thread — is whether she wants to learn the CAD side or stay in fabrication. Kwame says she has the hands for fabrication. Her cousin Ama says you should learn the design because then you own the thing end to end. Yaa doesn't know. She's good at both in the specific way of not being sure yet, which feels different from not being good.

What she knows: she likes when a piece comes off the bed clean. She likes the weight of it in her hand. She likes that the thing she makes goes somewhere — into someone's project, someone's repair, into whatever they're building that she'll never fully see.

She's eighteen. She has a whole opinion about the fans. She's still deciding about the rest.
