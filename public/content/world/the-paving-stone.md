The following Saturday Priya came down with the mortar and nobody was there yet. She left the bag by the door and went back up to get coffee.

By the time she came back Lior was crouching over the stone, pressing on the corners to see how bad it was. He'd brought a trowel. Nobody asked him to bring a trowel.

Kestner came out around ten with his kid, who immediately stepped on the loose stone and then stepped on it again. "How long has this been like this," Kestner said. It wasn't really a question.

They mixed too much mortar — more than they needed, more than was going to set before the air got to it — and Priya made a note of the ratio for next time, which felt optimistic but also maybe accurate. Lior did most of the actual work because he'd done it before, years ago, on something else. His kid helped by crouching next to him and handing him things he didn't ask for.

The stone went down solid around noon. Kestner stepped on it. Priya stepped on it. It didn't move.

Someone used the leftover mortar on a crack near the drain that they'd been walking past all winter. Nobody had put that on a list. It just seemed like a reasonable use of what was already mixed.
