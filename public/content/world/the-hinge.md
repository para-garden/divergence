The elevator was broken again — the handwritten note still on the door, same note as last week, maybe the same as forever. Daphne was on the stairs with a bag of components and a desoldering iron she'd wrapped in an old shirt.

Bob was already on the landing, holding two bags of groceries, waiting for something, looking at the door to the second floor which was apparently being difficult.

She got to the landing and he looked up and did the nod.

She did the nod back.

Then he said, "The trick is you have to lift and push at the same time," and she said "What?" and he demonstrated with one of the grocery bags, sort of lifting the handle while nudging the door with his hip, and it opened.

"Hinge is bent," he said.

"How long has it been like that?"

He considered. "Since October maybe."

She thought about October. "Yeah."

He went through and held it. She followed, because that's what you do. They stood in the second floor hallway for a second with nowhere to be yet.

"I'm Bob," he said.

"Daphne."

"Third floor."

"Yeah."

"I fix networks," he said, which was a weird thing to offer, and he seemed to know it was a weird thing to offer.

"I fix electronics." She lifted the wrapped desoldering iron slightly, which also did not explain itself.

"Cool," he said, which was probably the right word.

She went up the stairs. He went into his apartment. That was it. That was the conversation. On the other side of it, they'd spoken.
