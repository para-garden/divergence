The stall with the good eggs is the third one past the bread. You'll know it — there's a folding table with a handwritten sign that's been rained on enough times the ink bled into the wood grain underneath.

Tuesday's quieter than Saturday but the selection's better because the weekend crowd took the obvious stuff. The strawberries that are left are the small ones, which are the ones worth buying anyway.

The woman who does the herbs grows them in window boxes — you can tell because the bunches are small and the leaves are perfect. She ties them with whatever string she has. Today it's blue. Last week it was the kind that comes off a hay bale.

The noise is mostly footsteps and the clatter of somebody's metal cart on the uneven pavers and two people talking about something that happened at a building a few streets over. Not arguing. Just recounting.

Tomatoes aren't in yet. You can ask but they'll just gesture at the sky. Couple more weeks.

You bring your own bag or they sell you one for thirty cents that you'll use until it falls apart, which takes longer than you'd think.
