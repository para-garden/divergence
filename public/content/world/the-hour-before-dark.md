Something happens in the last hour of light that doesn't have a name in most languages or has too many — the specific quality of late afternoon into early evening when the light goes horizontal and warm before it goes.

The market is winding down but not closed. The vendors pack slowly, the ones left are the ones who've been there all day and aren't in a rush. A few people are still buying. The smell of the market at this hour is different from the morning smell — denser, riper, the vegetables that have been in the sun all afternoon.

The bakery is closed. Marta went home two hours ago.

The courtyard is filling in the way it fills in at this hour: the people who have been inside all day coming out because the light is doing what it does and you can feel it through the window calling you. Kofi with his bike. The Ferreira kids in their end-of-day mode, slightly fractious. Someone on the bench with a drink.

The tram goes past more full than it was this morning. People going somewhere for the evening or going home from being somewhere.

At some specific moment the light changes tone. You feel it before you see it. The city adjusts: a few more lights on in windows, the courtyard getting slightly dimmer, someone bringing something warmer to put on. Not cold yet. Just different.

Then the dark comes gradually and then all at once, which is how it always is, and the evening starts, which is its own thing.
