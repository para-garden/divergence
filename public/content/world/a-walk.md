She left at ten with no destination and let the city decide. This is possible when you know it well enough — you can navigate without navigating, follow interest, take the next street that looks like something rather than the one you usually take.

The neighborhood near the factory building first — she knows it but not by heart, there are always streets she hasn't taken. The building with the double doors was open. She looked in without going in: tools, bikes, the ghost of industrial height. Someone was working in the back.

She crossed toward the canal, which she goes to less than she should. The canal in March still has the winter smell — cold water, something mineral — and the banks are muddy where the path runs close. Two people were running, separately, in opposite directions. A dog was making itself very clear about something near the water.

Through a neighborhood she knows less well. The market here is different from hers — smaller, different vendors, a fruit she doesn't recognize and stopped to ask about. The vendor told her. She bought one. She ate it while walking, which was a mistake because it required two hands.

By the time she'd been walking an hour and a half the destination had assembled itself: the pool is near here, and it was a pool day, and there was time. She went. She swam. She hadn't planned to and the planning not to happen was part of what made it good.

She took the tram home because she'd been out long enough. The city from the tram window after walking through it: familiar differently, the way things look when you've recently been at the height of the pedestrian.
