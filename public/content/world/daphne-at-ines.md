Daphne arrived at seven with coffee she'd already made, which means she'd been planning to come. She didn't call ahead. She doesn't call ahead because she's been doing this for six years and Ines has never once seemed unwilling.

Ines was chopping something. She kept chopping. Daphne sat at the table.

This is how it mostly works. Ines talks and Daphne listens. Not performing listening — actually listening, which is a different thing. She's present in a way that some people find unnerving because they expect a response and she gives presence instead, which is better but harder to calibrate to.

Tonight Ines was working through something she'd been thinking about all week. One of the kids — Luca — had hit something in his understanding of mathematics that she'd been trying to figure out how to address. She talked through it, the approaches she'd tried, the ones she'd ruled out, the thing she kept coming back to. She talked for fifteen minutes, maybe twenty. The something got chopped and something else got started.

At some point she asked Daphne what she thought. Not rhetorical — actually asked.

Daphne thought for a moment. She said: *what if the problem isn't the approach, it's that he thinks there's supposed to be an approach.*

Silence. Then Ines said, quietly: *yeah.* And then immediately started talking again, faster, from a new direction.

Daphne drank the coffee she'd brought. Ines kept talking. This was the conversation they were having.

At nine Daphne left. Ines said thanks and meant it. Daphne said nothing in particular and meant that too.
