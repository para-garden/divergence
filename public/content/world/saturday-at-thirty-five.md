He slept until eight and didn't feel like he'd wasted anything. Made coffee. Stood at the window for a while.

He has things he wants to do today and things he has to do today and these are mostly the same things, which he didn't arrange on purpose. It's just where he ended up at thirty-five: the things he's chosen to do with his time have become the things he does, which is not as circular as it sounds. He chose them gradually, over years, by doing them when he didn't have to and finding out he liked them enough to keep doing them.

At ten he's at the workshop. Working on something that's been sitting at a particular problem for a week. He comes at it fresh today and finds the problem was smaller than it felt when he was tired; he resolves it in an hour. The rest of the morning is clear progress, the kind of progress you make when the blocking thing is gone.

He gets food at the market on the way home. Talks to the man with the apple stall briefly — about the apples, about the unseasonable warmth. Not a long conversation. Long enough.

The afternoon is his and he uses it the way he uses a Saturday afternoon: inefficiently, satisfyingly, doing whatever the moment suggests. Reading for a while. Going for a walk he didn't plan. Coming back. Looking at a thing he made three years ago that he still has opinions about.

At six there are people coming for dinner, which he's been looking forward to all week. He cooks. It doesn't need to be perfect. It needs to be food he made for people he wants to cook for, which is what it is.

The week will come back tomorrow. That's fine. Today was good.
