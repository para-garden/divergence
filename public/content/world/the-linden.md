The linden in the courtyard flowers in July. It always has. There's not much to say about it except that when it happens the courtyard smells like that for about two weeks, which is long enough to forget you're noticing it, and then it's over.

People have opinions about the linden. Specifically about which spring it leafed out earliest, and which year the flowers were most — something, nobody agrees on the word. More. Better. Fuller. A discussion that resurfaces every July and reaches no conclusion because there's no conclusion to reach.

It's not young. The base of it is wider than a person's armspan. Previous tenants of the building almost certainly had opinions about it too, and their opinions are not recorded anywhere, which is fine.

In October it turns. Yellow first, then something browner if it rains. Most of it comes down in a week when the weather decides to change. Tomás swept up once, which was a thing he did rather than a thing anyone asked him to do, and then the next day there were more.

The building doesn't have an official name. Some people call it the linden building, informally, to people who don't already know what building they mean.
