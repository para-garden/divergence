Fatou takes the Thursday slot. This week she needs to swap — her kid is sick, she can't go to the market, she needs someone to cover. She opens the tool, finds the week, finds the slot, looks at who's available for substitutions. There are three names. She messages one of them through the tool. The person accepts. The swap registers. The reminder for Thursday now goes to the other person.

This took four minutes. She doesn't think about the four minutes. She thinks about her kid.

The tool exists because someone made it. The someone was Noa, three cities over, who solved a scheduling problem for her building's food collective and then kept adding things when people needed things. Fatou doesn't know this. There's no reason she would. The tool works; that's what matters to Fatou.

Noa will get a log of the substitution. She might look at it or she might not — she's been watching for the race condition in simultaneous swaps, so she checks the logs occasionally. She'll see: Thursday slot, building collective, substitution successful. She won't know about the sick kid. She won't know Fatou's name unless she reads the log carefully, which she probably won't.

Two hundred people doing their food collective scheduling because someone fixed a broken spreadsheet five years ago and kept going. Fatou isn't one of the two hundred Noa knows roughly. She's one of the eighty or so who just quietly use it.

The kid's fever broke on Wednesday. Fatou went to the market Thursday anyway, covering the person who covered her, because the person who covered her had done her a favor and that's how it works.
