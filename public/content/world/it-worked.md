You know when it works. You don't have to check, but you check anyway, because checking is part of it.

The pot Tomás has been trying to get right for two months — the specific shape, the specific proportion, the foot ring that reads as intentional rather than compensatory. He pulled it from the kiln on Thursday and held it and turned it once and knew. He stood there with it for a moment longer than he needed to.

The section of code Noa had been circling for a week — the sync logic that kept having edge cases, that she kept thinking she'd fixed and then finding something wrong at the margin. Monday morning she sat down with it again and saw the shape of it differently, a different way to think about the state, and wrote it in an hour and it handled everything, including the cases she'd been fighting. She ran the tests three times. She knew after the first.

The wall section Jared had been looking at for three weeks. The upper right corner that wasn't doing the thing. He came out on the balcony on a Tuesday morning before work and looked at it and added something and then stepped back and it was there. He was going to be late; he stood there for a minute anyway.

No one was watching any of these moments. Nothing was at stake except the thing. The thing is what you're trying to do and when it does it, that's the whole experience: a specific, private, completely real satisfaction that doesn't need witnessing.

You go on to the next thing. But you keep the knowledge of having gotten that one right.
