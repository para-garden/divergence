The waiting room had a fish tank. Not a good fish tank — one of the small acrylic ones, slightly cloudy, two fish who didn't seem to know each other. She watched them while the man across from her read something on his phone and the woman next to him knitted something complicated.

Her knee had been doing a thing. Not a bad thing. A thing that happened when she went down stairs too fast, a small catch, a half-second of uncertainty. She'd noticed it for about three weeks before she remembered she could just come here.

The nurse called her name wrong — one syllable off — and she corrected it and he apologized and she said it was fine, which it was.

The doctor was maybe forty, had a kid's drawing of a horse taped to her monitor. She asked about the knee. She pressed it in a few places. She asked Sera to bend it, extend it, walk to the window and back.

"Probably the IT band. I want to see you do stairs."

They went into the hall. Sera went down four steps and back up.

"Yeah. Do you stretch in the mornings?"

"Not really."

"Start with that. If it's still catching in a month, come back and we'll look at it more carefully. Doesn't feel structural."

Sera said okay. She meant it. She put her jacket back on and scheduled a follow-up on her way out, mostly out of habit, half expecting she wouldn't need it.

Outside it was still the same Tuesday it had been before. She had errands.
