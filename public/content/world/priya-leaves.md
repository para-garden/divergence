Priya left on a Wednesday because the van she borrowed was free Wednesday.

She'd told the building in the way you tell a building — mentioned it to Kofi at the recycling bins, said something to the Delacroix kids when they asked why she was carrying boxes, texted the courtyard group at some point in the previous week. Nobody made an event of it. By Tuesday evening there were two bottles of wine on her kitchen counter that she hadn't put there, and a card from the third floor with everyone's signatures and one drawing of a cat that she didn't know the origin of.

The last morning she woke up early because the mattress was going in the van first and she had nowhere to sleep late. She made coffee in the pot she'd already packed and unpacked because she kept forgetting it was packed. Stood at the window with the cup. The courtyard was quiet. The linden hadn't leafed out yet. She'd been here for four springs and she had opinions about how the linden looked in each of them, a thing she didn't know she'd accumulated.

Tomás came out around eight and saw her loading and came over without making it a whole thing. He took one end of the couch. They talked while they worked — not about leaving, about the couch, about whether it would fit, about where she was going and what the kitchen situation was. The kind of conversation that happens over furniture.

By eleven the van was almost full and there was a loose cluster of people in the courtyard that hadn't been organized — Delacroix, the couple from the second floor whose name she'd never fully locked, the woman with the herb pots who she'd borrowed rosemary from four or five times. Standing in the sun. Someone had brought bread. It wasn't a party. It was just the building taking a moment.

She hugged people. She said she'd come back to visit. She would. The neighborhood was still here; she just wouldn't be in it.

The drive out she kept the windows down even though it was cold. The city went past. She'd learned it through walking and now she was moving through it too fast to read. She thought about the linden. She thought about how the light came through her old bathroom window at a specific angle in early morning that she'd never found a use for, just noticed.

She'd find what the new place had. That was the other side of this. It had a courtyard too, different geometry. Different linden or no linden. She'd accumulate opinions about that one.
