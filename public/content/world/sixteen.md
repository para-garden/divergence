He has nowhere to be until two, so he sleeps until nine and doesn't feel guilty about it. The morning belongs to him. He makes something to eat, checks what's on the shared screen in the building lobby, reads something for a while — not for anything, just because he wanted to.

At two he's at the workshop because he told Kwame he'd help with the inventory, which is boring but Kwame asked and it's an hour. He's been at the workshop long enough that Kwame trusts him with the boring things, which he understands is a form of being trusted.

After that: no plan. He goes by the market on the way home. Gets something. Texts two people, one responds, they end up in the courtyard for a while talking about nothing in particular. He's known these people for six years. They talk about the thing one of them is making, the thing the other one is thinking about doing, a thing they all watched last week.

At seven there's food because his mother made something. His father gets home late on Tuesdays. The meal is the three of them. They talk about their days. His day doesn't have a lot of content but neither does that require explanation; days can be light. His mother had a thing go wrong with a client that's boring to explain but she explains it anyway and he listens because she'd listen to his things.

Before bed: reads more, talks to someone online he knows from a thing he went to two years ago, plays something for a while without committing to it.

Tuesday. Nothing particular. He's sixteen and doing fine and doesn't know yet that this is the part people will later call simple, which will annoy him when they say it, correctly.
