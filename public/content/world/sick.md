He was sick for three days. The bad kind of sick — the lying-in-bed kind, the kind where the ceiling is too interesting because it's the only thing to look at.

He told work on Monday morning and that was the whole conversation. He slept. He drank things. He ate crackers when crackers were possible. The second day was the worst; the third was improvement; the fourth he was at about eighty percent and decided that was enough.

His downstairs neighbor brought soup. He hadn't asked. She knocked, left it outside the door, sent a message saying it was there. He ate it in bed and found it good in the specific way of things that are exactly what you need when you're sick. He sent a message back. She replied: *there's more if you want it.* He did want it, the next day.

He didn't worry about anything while he was sick because there was nothing to worry about that wouldn't still be there when he was better. Work would be there. Everything would be there. He was just sick, and then he would be better, and he was. This is how it was.

He thought about this a little bit on the fourth day when he was better enough to think about things. Not gratefully exactly — gratitude would have required comparison to something, and this is just how being sick works. He just thought: that was okay. Three days. He felt fine now.

He returned the soup container on Saturday with something he'd made to put in it.
