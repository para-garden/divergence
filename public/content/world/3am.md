The window was open a crack and the city had that sound it gets after two — not silent, never silent, but different. Someone's heat exchanger cycling somewhere. The fox again, probably. She'd seen it twice from this window, both times around now.

Mara pulled the blanket up without getting up and looked at the ceiling. The streetlight came in at an angle and made a pale bar across the plaster. She'd lived here four years and she still liked that bar. She didn't know why.

Not tired, not un-tired. The kind of awake where your body just isn't interested in sleep yet and you can either fight it or not.

She didn't.

The refrigerator clicked in the kitchen. Below, very faint, someone else was up — she could tell from a sound she couldn't even identify, just a quality to the building, the specific weight of two people awake in it versus one. The Salazars, maybe. They kept odd hours. It didn't bother her.

She thought about the bread. She'd started a loaf at ten and the kitchen smelled like it. Not a specific thought about the bread, just the bread existing in her mind the way objects do when you're in that state — present, uncomplicated.

At some point she heard the fox.

At some later point she was asleep.
