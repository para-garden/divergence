In August the pool is what you want it to be. The walk over is hot. The locker room is cool. The water is what it always is — they don't warm it in summer, the cold is a constant — and in August the cold is correct. You stand at the edge and then you go in.

The lanes are full by ten but not impossible. The rhythm of it: the person ahead of you, the person behind you, the particular mathematics of lane swimming where everyone is traveling at different speeds and the overtaking happens by feel. She's been doing this long enough that she knows when to draft and when to give way without thinking about it. The lane is a negotiation that runs on autopilot.

After twenty minutes she stops thinking about the heat outside. That's what it's for.

She comes out and the heat is immediately there again, which after the water is its own kind of good — the specific relief of having been somewhere cold and returning to warmth. She sits on the edge for a few minutes. The sun is high. The sounds of the pool around her: the water, the turns, the occasional child who is technically too young to be in this lane and is being tolerated because they're not causing problems.

She has coffee waiting in her bag, from the machine by the entrance. It's not good coffee. She drinks it every time.

The week continues. She comes back Thursday.
