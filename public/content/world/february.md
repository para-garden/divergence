February is just February. The light is still short. The first snow is long past and what's left is the gray version of winter — the cold that's familiar enough to not be interesting anymore, the trees that have been bare since October and will be bare for another month.

People have adjusted. The market has its winter inventory and people have stopped being surprised by it. The coat comes on without ceremony. The tram is warmer than the street in the way that makes you stay on it one stop past your stop if you're not paying attention.

The pool has a different feeling in February — something about the contrast of the cold walk and the warm water, the way the steam rises from the lanes. Renata swims. The regulars are there. Nobody mentions February particularly.

Somewhere in the second week there's usually a day that's almost warm. Not warm. The temperature the same as October but the light coming from a slightly different angle, and the body reads it as warmth, and for a few hours there's a loosening. People take their coats off five minutes too early. Then it goes back.

The linden is completely bare. Tomás says the linden always looks most like itself in February, stripped of everything — just the shape of it. He says this to people who ask about the courtyard and they nod and mostly don't see what he means until they've been here through a winter.

The forum has more posts in February. The pool has more people. Everyone is waiting for something and doing other things while they wait. That's winter. Then it turns, and spring comes the way it always comes: gradually and then all at once.
