The bakery changed hands twice in twenty years. The first change: Georg sold to Marta, and Marta was different from Georg — different hours, different bread, different relationship to the counter. That took a year to feel normal. It's normal now. The second change was the one that didn't happen: someone made an offer, Marta didn't take it. The neighborhood doesn't know about this.

The factory building got converted. Renata remembers it as a building she walked past that was closed. Now it has people in it and the ground floor opens in spring.

Two trees died. One elm on the west side, taken by disease; one linden in someone else's courtyard, reasons she doesn't know. The elms in general have had a difficult few years. The young ones planted to replace things are not yet at the height where they're useful for shade.

Three families she knew well moved away. Not in the same year — over time, for their various reasons. Some of them she still knows, in the way you know people when the proximity is gone. Others became names she associates with a specific floor or apartment. Two new families moved into buildings she'd watched stay empty. She knows them slightly.

What stayed: the pool. The forum. The market, though the vendors shift. The paving stones she's been walking on for so long she knows the uneven one by the pharmacy without looking. The light in the afternoon on the east wall. The cat situation, which is never the same cat but is always a cat.

The neighborhood is always changing and is always itself. She's been here long enough that she can see both things at the same time.
