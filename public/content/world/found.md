She'd left the key with her neighbor before she went on the trip. Neighbor moved in January. The key went with everything else that got handed back. She didn't notice until she was standing at her door at eleven at night with her bag and no key.

She wasn't angry. She wasn't even particularly stressed. It was solvable. Everything about this was solvable.

She messaged the neighbor, who felt terrible and messaged back immediately. The spare key was at her new place — she could bring it, twenty minutes. Come in, she said. Have something. Twenty minutes.

So she went to the new place, which was three streets over, and they had tea, and the former neighbor felt bad about the key and Selin said genuinely: it's fine, it's a key, it's eleven at night and I'm having tea with you, this is fine. And it was fine.

She got home at midnight. The key worked. The flat was the way she'd left it. She was more awake than she'd planned to be but less annoyed than she might have been, which is sometimes how things go when things go sideways in a small way and people are decent about it.

She put a spare key with the building's collective box, which she should have done years ago. Problem solved and then solved better.
