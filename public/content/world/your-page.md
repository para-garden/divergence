You get the page when you get the address. It's not a separate thing you sign up for. The address is the page is the place. You open it and it has almost nothing — your name if you registered with a name, a blank where things go.

Some people fill it in quickly. Some people come back to it months later. Some people look at it once and put their phone number and hours if applicable and leave the rest blank, which is also a choice, the page being yours includes the choice to leave it mostly blank.

The ones that are alive are the ones where someone decided: this is where I put that thing. Not "this is my presence." Just: this is where I put the list of plants I'm propagating, or the notes from the boiler repair, or the photographs I take of the neighborhood at different times of year. A specific purpose or no purpose, just a place.

The page has a font until you change the font. Nobody's stopping you from changing the font. A lot of people change the font once and then leave it. Some change it back. Some do something with the colors that is very much their own. The page ends up looking like whoever made it, which is the point, even though nobody called it the point.

You can link to other pages. You can embed things. You can make it as complex as you know how to make it or as simple as you're inclined to make. It doesn't go anywhere. It accretes. Years later, if you look back at the early version, you'll see something that was you before you knew what you were going to put there.

The page is yours. That's the thing. Not in the sense that someone is telling you it's yours. Just: it's yours.
