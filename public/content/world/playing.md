Someone has a guitar. Someone else has a thing — a small keyboard, a violin case, a voice. They're at a kitchen table or they're in a living room that's been slightly rearranged, and they're playing. Not rehearsing. Not performing. Just doing it.

The first few minutes are calibration — tuning, finding the key, the slightly awkward moment where you figure out whether you're playing the same thing. Then you find it. Or you find something adjacent and decide that's fine.

This works better when nobody's watching. Not that being watched ruins it, but when it's just the people playing, the playing is for itself. You stop when you lose it and start again. You play a section five times because the fifth time it does the thing you were trying for. You follow something interesting and end up somewhere you didn't expect and either decide to stay there or find your way back.

The building hears it through the walls. Usually nobody says anything. The building next door probably hears it too. The windows are open in summer.

They play for two hours or three hours and then they stop because someone has to go or the thing has run its course. They talk for a while after. The conversation is looser, less organized, the way conversation is after something that bypassed the verbal part.

Nobody recorded it. There's no record that this happened except that it happened. That seems exactly right.
