The bathroom floor is straightforward. Eight-by-eight tile, subway pattern offset, nothing fancy. The complication is the threshold — the hallway floor is wood and they want a clean transition, which means the tile has to end at the right height, which means the substrate prep matters more than it usually does.

He spent the first hour on that. Nobody watching would know what he was doing. He knows. The floor does.

By noon he had the field tile down and was cutting the wall edge. The table saw outside, the cuts holding, a rhythm. You get into it. Not a trance, more like a conversation where you're listening better than usual — the tool is saying something, the material is saying something back, you're adjusting.

He'll come back tomorrow for grout. The tile has to cure. This is the part people don't like — not the work, the waiting. He doesn't mind waiting when there's a reason.

He ate lunch in the stairwell because it was cooler than outside, looking at his phone a little, not really. Thought about the section of the wall he'd been looking at that morning. He's been circling something in the upper right corner for two weeks. Not sure what. You can't force that.

Finished at four. Walked home with some grout dust in his hair that he didn't notice until he caught himself in the mirror. The work day had a shape. The painting doesn't have a shape, which is why you do both.
