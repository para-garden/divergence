The tool Noa made is used in forty-three cities now. She knows this because people tell her. Some of them she's talked to. Most she hasn't. The tool traveled through a specific path — person tells person, collective talks to collective — and ended up where it ended up, which is further than she was thinking about when she was building a food schedule for her building.

This is how things move between cities here. Not through a platform that optimizes for spread. Through people, and the people they talk to, and the things those people say. It's slow by some measures. By other measures it's fast enough — the right thing reaches the right people because those people are looking for it.

A specific repair technique that someone figured out in the smaller city arrived in Renata's building via a message thread that went: person in smaller city to their friend in the capital, who mentioned it to someone in the building, who mentioned it to Renata, who mentioned it in her page. Four hops, two weeks, the information now in the building's collective knowledge.

A piece of music made in a city three train hours away became something people in this city knew. Not instantly. Not because it was pushed at them. Because someone went there and came back and played it for people who heard it and played it for other people.

Cities are not closed. They're not connected by a network that flattens the distance between them — they're connected by people who move between them and carry things. The carrying is slower. What arrives arrives because it was worth carrying.

Fatou goes home to Dakar every two years and comes back with things her city doesn't have. Yusuf came from somewhere else and brought things people here didn't know they'd find interesting. The exchange is continuous and personal and irreducible.
