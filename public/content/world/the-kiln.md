Tomás has a rule about the kiln: you stay while it fires. Fire safety, he said once, and I believed him, and also I think he just likes having someone there.

I brought a board I've been meaning to reflow for two weeks. He didn't ask what it was. I sat at the end of the worktable with my hot air station and he sat at his wheel with something low and wide taking shape under his hands, and for an hour there was just the kiln's background heat and the whine of my station and occasional clay sounds.

At some point he set a cup down next to me without saying anything. I drank it.

The board turned out fine — a bad cap, nothing interesting. I packed it back up and watched him work for a while. His hands know things. I know my hands know things too but watching it in someone else is different, you see the knowing from the outside.

"You should try it," he said, not looking up.

"I've tried it."

"You made a lopsided cylinder."

"I made a functional lopsided cylinder."

He almost smiled. The clay kept moving.

I didn't leave when the reflow was done. I could have — there wasn't a reason to stay except that the kiln was warm and it was a tuesday and he was there. When the cycle finished he checked the door seals, wrote something in his log, and said he was going to get food, did I want to come. I wanted to come. We went.

Ines would make this mean something. She'd put a word on it and hand it back to me wrapped up. I know what she'd say: *you're never this still anywhere else.* Which, maybe. But Ines makes everything a signal. I'm not a signal. I was cold and the kiln was warm and he put a cup down without asking if I wanted one, which is a thing you do when you already know.
