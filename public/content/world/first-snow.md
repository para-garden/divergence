The snow started overnight so by morning it was just already there. A fact. Three or four centimeters — enough to cover the courtyard, not enough to cause anything.

The linden looked different. Heavier and also quieter somehow, which is a thing snow does to trees. The bench that usually has someone on it by eight-thirty didn't, for a while. Then it did, because someone came out in a coat and sat on it anyway with coffee and just looked at the courtyard the way you look at something when it's briefly different.

The 14 ran slow. Not stopped — slow, careful, the way drivers get when they're being responsible about it. The city rerouted itself, not dramatically, just everyone making small adjustments at the same time without coordinating. More people in the bakery than usual, standing longer at the counter, not in a rush to go back out.

The snow on the pavement went fast — too warm for it to stay. The streets were wet by noon. But the rooftops held it most of the day, and the small field two blocks over still had a white coat at four, with some marks across it where something had walked through. Fox-sized, probably. The city not entirely solid underfoot.

By evening it was just cold.
