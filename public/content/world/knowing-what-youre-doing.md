After enough time, the doing changes shape. Not that you do it better, though you do — it's that you stop consulting yourself about it. The question isn't *how do I do this,* the question is *what is this asking for.* Those are different questions.

Marta doesn't think about how to shape a loaf. She's shaped enough loaves that the information has moved somewhere else, into her hands, into the part of her that isn't verbal. If you asked her how she does it she'd have to slow down and watch herself and reconstruct it, which would make her worse at it temporarily. You stop knowing how and start knowing what.

The same thing with the tile. With the handles. With the code that's doing a job nobody thinks about because it does the job right. You build up a debt to the years and the years pay out in this: you're no longer spending attention on the mechanics. The attention can go elsewhere — to the thing that's different today, the problem that's new, the moment where you need to be present because the usual answer won't do.

People notice this from the outside as something calm. From the inside it doesn't feel like anything. It's not calm, it's just competence, which is quiet the way that not having a problem is quiet.

Renata refinished the cabinet door. She did it the way she's done it every time. It came out right. She doesn't have a feeling about this. She hung it and checked the level and made coffee.

There will be a next time and she'll do it that way too.
