He was bored on Sunday. Properly bored — not restless in a way he could solve by doing anything in particular, just bored, the specific weight of having time and no use for it.

He tried reading. He couldn't read. He tried something on a screen. He closed it after twenty minutes. He stood in the kitchen for a while doing nothing.

This is fine. Boredom is fine. It's not a problem to solve. He's old enough to know this and still found it slightly annoying because knowing something doesn't stop the feeling.

At some point he went for a walk, not because he wanted to but because being in the flat was worse. He came back. He made food. He ate it standing up at the counter and then moved to the table, which was better.

By evening it had passed. Not because anything happened — it just ran its course. He watched something, genuinely this time. He went to bed at a reasonable hour.

Monday the week started and there was too much to do and he thought, briefly, about Sunday when there had been nothing to do. Both seemed fine in retrospect. The boredom had a quality of rest to it that he hadn't seen while he was in it — the doing-nothing had been genuine nothing, not nothing with an edge of anxiety underneath. That's the thing about boredom when there's nothing threatening it: it's just bored. And then it ends. And then you have dinner.
