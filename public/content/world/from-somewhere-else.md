The tram was the first thing that felt like home. Not because it was the same tram — it wasn't, different city, different line, different color — but because the way you wait for it is the same everywhere. The way you stand slightly apart from the person beside you but aware of them. The way you move for someone who needs to get through.

She'd been here eight months before she stopped translating in her head. Not the language — her language worked fine here, or well enough — but the city. Before, she was always doing a conversion: *this is like the market at home but smaller, this is like the neighborhood where my aunt lives but with different trees.* At some point she stopped converting and the city was just the city.

The food took longer. Not finding food she recognized — she could find it, or close enough, or something new she learned to like. The thing that took longer was the specific combinations, the things that meant a particular meal with particular people on a particular occasion. You can make the food. The occasion is harder.

She has people here now. Not the same people — different people, people who know a version of her that arrived already formed and doesn't include the eighteen years before. She's explained some of it. They've listened. There are things she hasn't explained because they'd take too long or land wrong without context.

She calls her mother on Sundays. The time difference is manageable. Her mother asks about specific things — does she have enough of certain things, is there a particular kind of shop nearby — and she answers specifically. The specific questions are the love. Her mother knows this, she knows her mother knows this.

This city has a linden in the season. That's the same. She didn't know she'd have opinions about linden trees and now she does.
