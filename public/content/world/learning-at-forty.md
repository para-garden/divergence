She started learning to throw clay at forty-two. Not because of anything — nobody suggested it, it wasn't a resolution, she'd walked past the workshop and seen Tomás and thought: I've always been curious about that.

So she went. She told him she was curious and he said okay and gave her the Wednesday evening slot.

She's been going for eight months. She's not good. She knows she's not good; she can see what good looks like and she's not there. What she is: improving. Slowly, with the specific slowness of learning something physical when you're past the age where your hands learn things quickly.

What surprises her: she doesn't find the not-good-yet part humiliating. She thought she might. She is a person who is competent at the things she does, and she expected to find incompetence distressing. Instead she finds it straightforwardly interesting. She makes something, it's wrong, she figures out what's wrong, she does it again. That's just the loop.

Tomás said she has good patience. She took this as the compliment it apparently is. He's sparing with this kind of thing.

She has three objects she's made and kept. One is an actual cup that works, she uses it. One is a small bowl that's lopsided in a way she decided is fine. One is something that was going to be something and became something else and she's not sure what it is but she kept it.

Her partner asked when she'll be good at it. She said she didn't know and also didn't particularly mind. He thought about this and then nodded.
