She's usually asleep when he leaves in the morning. He works early. He tries not to make noise with the coffee but the grinder is what it is, so she's half-awake by the time he's heading out, and he leans in the doorway for a minute and she says something that is approximately a word, and that's fine, that's enough.

He's asleep when she gets in. She works late — not every night, but often enough that quiet has become her default way of being in the flat after ten. She knows which boards creak. She heats up whatever's left or makes something cold and sits at the table in the kitchen light.

They have meals on days off. He makes eggs in a specific way — too much butter, she's accepted this — and she makes the coffee because his is bad. These are known things. The eggs take a while because he's not in a hurry on days off and she's learned to not be in a hurry on days off too, which took time.

Neither of them is good at remembering to text during the day. This sometimes creates uncertainty about dinner. They've decided this is the shape they are and have stopped trying to change it.

On evenings when they're both in and not tired — these happen — they're on the couch or at the table, doing their own things in the same room. The flat is small enough that being in different rooms means being alone. They're mostly not in different rooms.

She comes home late on a Thursday and he left something on the stove for her. She sends him a message. He's asleep. He sees it in the morning.
