Not the earned kind. Not the satisfaction of something finished or figured out. The other kind: the kind that arrives without notice and lands on you before you can prepare.

The linden flowering in July when you walk into the courtyard and the smell hits you — not that it's a surprise, it happens every year, but every year it arrives before you've remembered to expect it.

A song you haven't heard since you were seventeen, playing from someone's window as you walk past, and the twenty intervening years collapse for a moment into the feeling of being seventeen and hearing it for the first time except you're not seventeen and you're in the street and you stop walking without meaning to.

The moment at the end of a long week when you sit down and there's nothing you have to do — not the relief of the long week ending, the specific clean weightlessness after the relief, the unexpected extra lightness on top of the expected rest.

A fox in the afternoon, which isn't when you expect foxes.

Bea's face when the box lid fit. She didn't show it; she's ten and it isn't cool to show it. She showed it. Tomás saw. He said nothing.

These don't require the floor. The floor doesn't cause them. What the floor does is leave them unmixed — delight without the background hum of what you're managing, what you're calculating, how much of yourself is available for the arrival of a fox or a song or a smell. The channel is clearer. What comes through comes through.
