The shelf holds weight. Bea knew it would but she wanted to know for sure, so she tested it by standing on it, which wasn't the kind of test Ines had in mind but also wasn't wrong.

Since then she's made two more things: a small stool that wobbles slightly, which she's decided is acceptable, and a box with a lid that fits on the first try, which she was privately very pleased about and told nobody except Tomás who was in the workshop at the time.

She has opinions now about grain direction. She can't fully explain them yet — she knows the grain matters, she knows she's seen what happens when you work against it, she doesn't yet have the language for all of it. Kwame at the workshop has been talking her through it. She listens in the way that kids listen when they actually want to know something, which is different from the way they listen when they're supposed to.

She's ten. She still goes to Ines's sessions on Tuesdays and Thursdays. The other days she is in various places doing various things, a proportion of which involve figuring out how things are put together. Not all of them. She watches things on screens. She plays with the Ferreira kids in the courtyard. She is not only interested in how things work, she is just also interested in how things work.

The stool wobbles. She could fix it. She's deciding.
