The stalls change before you consciously notice them changing. One week the tomatoes are still there. The next week they're not, or they are but fewer, or the ones that are there are smaller and the vendor shrugs at you in a way that means *this is how it goes*. The zucchini disappear. The peppers are there but not the green ones. And then one morning you arrive and there are parsnips, actual parsnips, and you think: oh. October.

Crates of apples from somewhere specific — the kind that bruise if you look at them wrong, not the kind that lasts six months in a warehouse. Squash in shapes that don't fit neatly. Fennel in quantities that suggest someone grew a lot of fennel. Someone is always growing a lot of fennel and hoping for the best.

The light is lower. The sellers have an extra layer on. The smell is different — less green, more something else, soil and sweetness and the edge of rot that comes with ripeness.

People buy more at once in October. Root vegetables keep. There's something that shifts in people when the season does, a recalculation toward storing, even if they're not thinking about it in those terms. You stock up on things you can actually keep. You buy the fennel even though you're not sure what you'll do with all of it.

On the way out there are persimmons from the tree on the corner road — a few, whatever the tree gave this year, no more. Someone has a sign with the price. Someone else has already bought half of them.
