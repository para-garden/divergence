Her daughter takes the 9:40 and is here by eleven. Renata knows this and still listens for the door. The coffee is ready; she made too much coffee, as always, in the way of people who grew up when coffee meant something about hospitality and haven't unlearned this.

They sit at the kitchen table. The cabinet door is finished and hung — it hangs level, she checked twice — and her daughter says *it looks good* and Renata says *it was just the refinishing, the cabinet itself is fine* and this is the kind of conversation they have, practical, comfortable, old. They know each other well enough that the conversation doesn't have to try.

They eat lunch. She made enough for lunch. Her daughter tells her about work — she's doing something with water treatment, Renata understands parts of it, the parts she doesn't understand she asks about and gets explanations that are better than the first one because her daughter knows her well enough to adjust. Her daughter tells her about the people she lives with, the flat situation, a disagreement about the hallway that was resolved and is not interesting but they talk about it anyway.

Renata tells her about the building meeting. About the alcove extension. She has an opinion about the compromise. Her daughter has the same opinion, basically, which is satisfying, but she interrogates it anyway — *but what's Marek's argument, specifically?* — because she wants to know she's thinking about it and not just feeling about it.

At four her daughter leaves for the train. Renata goes back to the kitchen. The coffeepot has residue. She cleans it the way her mother cleaned it.

Next week or the week after. Depending on work. That's fine. There's the page, and there are the messages, and there are these Thursdays when they manage.
