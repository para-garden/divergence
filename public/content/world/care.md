Three visits today. The first at eight-thirty: an older man, post-surgery, getting back his mobility, the exercises going well, he's stubborn about them in the useful direction. She checks the wound, checks his movement, talks with him for fifteen minutes because he lives alone and the visit is partly for the wound and partly for the fifteen minutes, and she knows this and doesn't make it weird by saying so.

Second visit: a woman with a chronic thing, managing not curing, the conversation mostly about the managing — what's working, what isn't, what needs adjusting. She knows this woman well enough to know when she's being optimistic about compliance. She's direct about it without making it a scolding. The woman makes a face that means *yes fine, you're right.* That's fine.

The third is the one she finds hardest. Not the complexity — it's medically uncomplicated — but the family situation, the dynamics in the room when she arrives. She does the care. She doesn't get drawn into the dynamics. She says what needs to be said directly to the person she's there for. This takes practice.

The fourth was cancelled. She rescheduled it for Thursday. She has the gap now and uses it to eat something and write her notes from the morning before the afternoon starts.

This is what it is. It's not complicated to explain: you go where people are, you tend to what needs tending, you come back tomorrow. The conditions that let someone do this work without calculating what it costs them to be a person at the same time as being the person who shows up — those conditions aren't visible from inside the work. They're just the ground.

She eats her lunch. The afternoon starts at two.
