The linden flowers on a Tuesday in July. It flowers every July; this Tuesday it reached whatever it was building toward, the smell arriving in the courtyard overnight and being there in the morning.

Marta closed at two. She walked home through the part of the neighborhood she doesn't usually walk through, for no reason, because the day was good and she had time and the street was there.

Bea finished a joint she'd been working on at the workshop. She held it up. The grain was right. She showed Tomás. He looked at it for a second and said: *good.* That was enough.

Bob's monitor was running something he'd put on for himself. He looked at the courtyard through the window at around five and it was doing what it does in July at five — a few people, the linden smell, the light at the angle that makes everything look particular. He left the window open.

Noa fixed a small thing in the calendar system that had been bothering her for two weeks. It was a Tuesday, so she had the maintenance session scheduled. The fix was six lines. She tested it, closed the file, made coffee.

The courtyard in the evening: the usual accumulation without arrangement. Someone had bread. Someone else had wine. The Ferreira kids were cycling through, and then not, and then again. Lila found the paving stone that doesn't wobble and stood on it for a while and then went in.

Renata sat by her window until ten. The smell was still there at ten. She went to bed.

Nothing particular. Everything present. A Tuesday in July in a place where Tuesdays in July are allowed to be what they are.
