The gutter at the north corner had been sagging since before winter. Saturday morning, Bob noticed it from his window — the bracket had pulled away from the fascia, probably when the ice expanded — and went down to look.

Jared was already in the courtyard with a coffee, looking at it from below.

"I've got a ladder," Bob said.

"I've got screws," Jared said. "Somewhere."

Daphne came out twenty minutes later to water her balcony pots and saw them up there, Bob on the ladder, Jared holding it steady and handing things up. She went back inside and came back with a tube of exterior sealant she'd had since she re-caulked her window frame in October. "This help?"

"Yeah, actually," Bob said.

Marta came out to take her recycling and ended up staying. She held the gutter steady from the other end while Bob got the bracket seated again. Nobody asked her to. She just saw the problem.

The whole thing took maybe forty minutes. There was a period in the middle where they were all standing around waiting for the sealant to tack, nobody quite leaving, talking about the paving stone near the east door that had been uneven since the spring thaw. That one would need more than four people and a ladder. Someone should get the mortar. Maybe next weekend. Probably next weekend.

Bob came down. Jared found the cap for the sealant tube. Marta went back inside. Daphne went back up to her plants.

The bracket held.
