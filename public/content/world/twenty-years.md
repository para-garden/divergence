They've had most of the arguments already. Not resolved them all — some arguments are load-bearing and you just know where they are and navigate around them. But the new arguments are rare. Most things that come up are variations on arguments they've already had, and they both know it, and this takes some of the heat out.

Twenty-three years, technically. They've stopped tracking it precisely. Some anniversary in October — the day they technically decided, or the day they moved in together, there are two possible dates and they've given up choosing which one counts.

The mornings are practiced. Who makes coffee, who reads what, who takes up too much of the table with a project that has no business being at the breakfast table. The second thing is the same person every time. They've accepted this. It's part of the landscape now.

They've changed since they started. This is just true. The people they were in their late twenties are people they can describe but don't fully recognize. They find this mostly interesting — they compare notes sometimes, *you used to do this,* and the other person usually can't believe it. They've been witnesses to each other's becoming.

Hard things happened. Parents died, work changed, a health thing that took a year to work through. The floor being solid didn't remove the hard things. It meant they went through them without simultaneously managing the financial calculations, which is a different kind of going through them. They don't think about this as a distinction. It's just what they know.

On Sundays they eat lunch late. Not every Sunday. Most Sundays. They sit at the table and there's something to eat and they talk about whatever they talk about, which is nothing particular and also the ongoing project of being these two people.
