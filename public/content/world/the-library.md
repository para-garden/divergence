The library has been here longer than anything else on this block. The building was something else first — records suggest a warehouse, though the records themselves are in the library and haven't been organized in a way that makes this easy to verify. It became a library because it was large and it was there.

Three floors. Ground floor: the newer collection, the things people actually ask for, the section where someone left a note on the inside cover of a book that someone else found interesting enough to leave a response. Second floor: the older things, organized by a system that made sense to someone forty years ago and still mostly makes sense. Third floor: the archive and the space where people work on things — long tables, natural light from the big windows, a section with tools you can borrow.

People use it the way they use it. For books. For the tools. For the tables on days when their flat is too small for what they're doing. Noa comes sometimes with her laptop, sits in the third-floor light, works for a few hours. Clara from the Ferreira kids comes for the science section, which is good, and stays longer than she planned. Renata has a card from before the current system and they keep it active.

There's a board on the ground floor where people pin things — events, things found, things needed, questions that are general enough to be worth asking publicly. The board gets dense in spring for some reason. Someone puts up a question about a plant variety. Someone answers it with a piece of paper torn from a notebook.

The person at the ground-floor desk knows where things are and knows what to say when they don't. She's been there eight years. She learned the forty-year-old system.
