Three weeks, give or take. She'd been noticing the absence without deciding to notice it — the courtyard she doesn't belong to, the man whose name she doesn't know, the lunch he ate outside regardless of weather.

She'd thought about it intermittently. Not with worry, exactly. Something like a low-level question she hadn't asked out loud and had no way to ask.

On a Wednesday in November he came back out. She didn't catch it happening — she was working and glanced up and there he was, the usual spot, a plate, the winter light. He had a coat on that she hadn't seen before. He sat for the usual amount of time. He went back inside.

She noticed, and then she returned to the problem she'd been circling, and the noticing settled into a place where she wouldn't think about it unless something reminded her. That's how it goes. You observe a thing over years, you have one small worry about the thing, the thing resolves, and you go back to the ordinary watching without the worry in it.

She didn't know what the three weeks had been. She still doesn't. Sick, probably. Or something that required being inside, or a change in his schedule that ran until it didn't. It's a courtyard she can see from her window. It's not her courtyard. The resolution was just: he came back out.

That was enough.
