Daphne came back from the Bertrands' on Tuesday and Ines was at her table grading something, and Ines looked up and said "How'd it go?"

"Fine. They replaced the capacitors themselves. I just checked the work."

"They pay you for that?"

"Half rate. It's fine."

Ines put her pen down. "You've done that three times now."

"Done what."

"Gone out to check someone else's work. For half."

"It was a learning thing. They wanted to learn."

"Right, and you taught them, which is great, and then they paid you half because they did the easy part." Ines wasn't angry yet. She was laying it out. "That's not generosity, Daphne. That's you deciding their discomfort about owing you is worth more than your time."

"I didn't experience it that way."

"I know you didn't."

Daphne set her bag down. "Okay."

"I'm not lecturing you."

"You're doing the thing where you explain what I'm actually doing."

"Because you're not going to."

"Because I don't think I'm doing anything." She got a glass of water. Stood at the counter. "I like that they wanted to learn. I like that they tried. The half-rate was their idea and I said yes because it felt fair."

"It doesn't feel fair to me."

"Then it's lucky it's not your situation."

That landed wrong and they both knew it. Ines picked her pen back up, put it down again. "I'm going to say something."

"You've been saying things."

"This is different." She looked at her. "You do this with Tomás too. You find a way to make your needs smaller so everyone fits."

"That's not what I do with Tomás."

"You don't have to defend it right now."

"I'm not defending it, I'm telling you you're wrong." Daphne's voice had gone flat in the way it did. "What I do with Tomás is my business and it doesn't look like what you think it looks like from the outside."

"I know what it looks like from the outside because you've told me things. And I'm telling you there's a pattern."

"There's always a pattern if you're looking for one."

"Daphne."

"What."

Ines didn't say anything for a moment. "I'm not trying to be right about you. I'm telling you something I see because I've known you for six years and I care what happens to you. That's the only reason I'm saying it."

"I know that."

"Do you."

"Yes. And I still think you're wrong."

"Okay."

"Don't *okay* me like that."

"How would you like me to okay you."

Daphne laughed, short and involuntary. "I don't know. Less resigned."

"I'm not resigned. I said what I think. You said what you think. That can just be where we are."

"It can."

Neither of them said anything. Ines picked up her pen. Daphne stayed at the counter finishing her water.

"The Bertrands asked if I'd do a session with their daughter," Daphne said. "The older one. She wants to learn soldering."

"Are you going to?"

"Probably."

"And what are you charging."

"My rate."

"Okay," Ines said, and this time it was different.
