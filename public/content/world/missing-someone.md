Her mother died in February. Not unexpectedly — it had been coming for a year, the kind of coming that prepares you and doesn't prepare you. She got there in time. She came back in March. The flat was the same and she was different in a way that's hard to put into language.

Grief is grief. That part doesn't change.

What she has: time, when she needs it. No calculation about whether she can afford to be not-okay for a while. The leave she needed she got without explaining why except that it was her mother, which was enough. Her people came around in the way her people come around — food, presence, not requiring her to perform anything. She didn't have to be fine while she wasn't fine.

She went to work when she could go to work. She didn't when she couldn't. The gap between those two states moved gradually. She couldn't have told you on any given day exactly where she was in it.

In April she went back to where her mother lived. Sorted the flat — her brother was there too, they did it together, it was hard and also there were moments where they found something funny about an object and laughed in a way that felt complicated. Her mother had strong opinions about organization. The flat reflected this. They put things in order the way they thought she'd want them and it felt like a last conversation.

She's home now. It's June. She still wakes up sometimes thinking she should call her.

This is what grief does. It doesn't stop when the worst part is over. It just changes shape.
