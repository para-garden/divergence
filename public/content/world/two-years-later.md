They hadn't been in the same place for two years. Not a falling-out. Just distance, work, the way time moves when nobody is actively maintaining proximity and life keeps going. They'd messaged here and there. Not often. Enough to not be strangers.

He came through the city for something else. She suggested dinner. He said yes.

Ten minutes for the recalibration — the first ten minutes of seeing someone you haven't seen in a long time, where you're both remembering how you are with each other. Then it came back, or it was always there and the distance had just been distance. Either way, by the time the food came they were talking the way they used to talk.

He'd changed. She noticed it before she could name it. Something had settled in him that wasn't there before — not worse or better, just more resolved. He would have said the same about her, probably. You change and people who knew you before can see it more clearly than you can.

They stayed for three hours. Talked about the two years, incompletely — you can't compress two years into three hours, you do what you can. Talked about what they were thinking about now. Got into an argument about something she'd mostly forgotten fifteen minutes after they left.

On the way home she thought: that's a person I'm glad exists. She'd probably tell him this someday. Or she'd see him again in two years and it would still be true without anyone saying it.

He sent a message when he got back to his city. She replied in the morning.
