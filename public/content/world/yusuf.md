Yusuf's been in the building for three years. He's one of those people the building knows the way it knows a tree — by presence and pattern, not by much biographical information. He's there. He's generally fine. He'll come out for things.

He works in something to do with building systems — insulation assessments, the kind of work that's invisible when it's working. He's good at it in the way you're good at something that mostly involves knowing how things fail: you learn to look for the signs before the failure. He spends a lot of time in other people's walls.

He cooks for the courtyard sometimes. Not always. But if there's a gathering and he has something ready, he'll bring it down and put it on the table. He doesn't announce this. He just does it and then the thing is there. This is characteristic.

He went to the coast last September. He went alone, which is not unusual for him. He comes back from those trips slightly different — not changed, just recalibrated, the way certain people need a certain kind of quiet to reset. He told Kofi about the sea, standing in the courtyard, one evening. Kofi said something about having never liked the sea much. Yusuf laughed. They left it at that.

He knows the film nights are on the shared schedule. He comes when he comes. He brought the tomatoes that time, the bowl with the rooftop tomatoes plus whatever else was in the kitchen. Someone said *those look good.* He said thanks.

That's more or less how he is.
