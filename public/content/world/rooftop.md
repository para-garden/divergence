There are tomatoes on the roof because someone wanted tomatoes. Not a program. Not an initiative. Kofi brought up soil in bags over the course of a few weeks — he has a specific way of moving things that involves not rushing — and built four beds out of materials that were going to go in a skip, and planted in April, and now it's August and there are tomatoes.

Other people have added things. The bed at the far end is Renata's herbs — rosemary, two kinds of thyme, something she calls marjoram that has been going for three years without anyone asking what it is. The Ferreira kids planted sunflowers in May and the sunflowers are enormous and not producing tomatoes but everyone agrees they've earned their space.

You come up when you need something or when you don't. The view from here is the city — the low skyline of the neighborhood, the taller buildings beyond, the hills further out that you don't always notice from street level. People come up to sit sometimes even when they don't need anything from the beds.

Kofi picks in the morning before it gets hot. He leaves things out — a bowl, whatever's ripe — and people take what they need. This system has never been discussed. It functions because nobody decided to abuse it and there's enough for everyone and no one needed to optimize it for that reason.

By September the tomatoes slow down. He'll clear the beds in October, amend the soil, and start thinking about what to put in for next year. He doesn't know what it'll be yet. Maybe the same thing, maybe different.

The sunflowers are still going.
