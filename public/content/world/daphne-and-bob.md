The kettle in Bob's flat stopped heating. Not a difficult thing. Daphne came down when he mentioned it, looked at the element, determined that the thermal fuse had gone, found one in a bag of components she keeps for exactly this, replaced it. Twelve minutes including the time to find the right size.

She didn't make it a big thing and neither did he. He said thanks, she nodded, she went back upstairs. That was the first one.

After that they'd sometimes end up on the landing at the same time — her coming back from the market, him stepping out for air — and they'd talk for a few minutes or they wouldn't, depending on whether there was something to say. No system for it. Just the landing doing what landings do.

He asked her once about the plants on the sill, specifically the trailing one that had made its way toward the courtyard. She explained about the light without assuming he was asking for an explanation — just answered what he actually seemed curious about. He found this notable. A lot of people explain at you when you ask a question. She answered it.

She noticed he left the courtyard screen open for anyone to cast to. She found this a reasonable thing to do with a wall.

They don't eat together or make plans. If they're both in the courtyard they'll sometimes end up near each other. He'll say something about the screen. She'll say something back. The linden is usually doing something. That's mostly how it goes.
