The building meeting about the east wall extension took two sessions because the first one ran out of time. Fourteen people in the courtyard, folding chairs, the second Thursday of October, the linden mostly bare by then.

The proposal was straightforward: the storeroom on the ground floor could expand into part of the east wall's alcove, which would double the storage available to the building. The people who wanted this pointed out that the storeroom was full and bikes were ending up in the stairwell. The people who weren't sure about it pointed out that the alcove was where afternoon light came through, and the Ferreira kids used the alcove for various reasons, and the geometry of the courtyard would change.

Nobody was wrong. That was the thing about it. The storeroom really was full. The light through the alcove really was the light through the alcove.

A third position: compromise the extension to half-depth, which would add storage without changing the light much. A fourth position: fix the storeroom organization first, then see if the space was still needed. Kofi, who tends to be the one who notices when a meeting is getting longer than it needs to, said both of these were worth trying before committing to anything structural.

The second session started from the compromise. Three hours and a fair amount of coffee later they had a plan: try the half-depth version, two-year review.

Not everyone was fully happy. Marek thought the half-depth was too timid. Renata thought two years was too long to wait for a review. These opinions were noted. The meeting ended around nine. Someone brought the rest of the bread.

The alcove is still the alcove. The storeroom has a proposed extension. Winter came and the alcove looked the same.
