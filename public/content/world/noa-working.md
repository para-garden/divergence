A message came in Tuesday from someone she doesn't know well — they manage the Thursday food slot for a collective three cities over, they've been using the calendar tool for eight months, and there's a case in the substitution logic that doesn't behave right when two people try to swap the same slot at the same time. She looked at it. She could see where it went wrong. It's a race condition in the confirmation step — the tool wasn't built thinking about simultaneous requests because the first version was for one building's food schedule, and nobody swaps slots simultaneously in one building.

She fixed it in about forty minutes. Sent a message back saying it was fixed and what the issue had been. Got a reply that said *thank you, this is a lifesaver*. She's not sure a food-schedule race condition qualifies as a lifesaver but she understands what they mean.

This is most of it. A thing is wrong, she looks at it, it stops being wrong. The person on the other end usually doesn't know much about what she did and doesn't need to. The slot-swap works now.

In the afternoon she started something new — there's a building that wants a shared maintenance log, similar to the repair notes tool she already made but with more visibility into what's pending versus done. She's thinking about what that actually needs before she writes anything. She has a piece of paper with three words on it. The words are: *pending, done, who*.

The courtyard across the way is quiet. The man with two jackets still hasn't been out for lunch. She's noticed this three times now. She doesn't know what to do with the noticing. Probably nothing.
