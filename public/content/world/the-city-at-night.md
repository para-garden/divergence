The deli closed at nine but the light is still on inside — someone cleaning the case, probably, or restocking. A person stops at the window, sees the closed sign, keeps walking.

The conversation that's been going on outside the wine bar since eight has migrated to the corner. Four people. One is gesturing. The others are mostly listening, occasionally not.

Somewhere upstairs, music. Bass you can feel in your sternum. Nobody seems bothered.

A cat crosses the street without ceremony. Doesn't look. It's not a near-miss. There's just not much to miss.

The pavement is still warm from the day. The restaurant that puts tables outside stopped seating an hour ago but two people are still at one, wine in hand, nobody asking them to leave.

Two kids on the corner with their bikes, not going anywhere. Leaning. Talking. Old enough to be out, young enough that someone in one of the windows is keeping count.

The light is from everywhere — windows, storefronts still lit, the bar's open door — and from nothing in particular. No single source strong enough to wash everything out. Eyes adjust. The street has depth.

A door opens. Closes. Somebody's going home. Somebody else is just arriving.
