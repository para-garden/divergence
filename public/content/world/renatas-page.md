The page has been there since before Renata understood what she was doing with it. She started it when the tools became the floor — you had a place, it was writable, so you wrote in it. At first it was functional: she put her phone number and her hours on it. Then she added the thing about the building's radiators because she'd figured it out and thought someone else might need to know. Then more things, gradually, because the page was there and she was thinking about things.

It doesn't look like other pages. The font is too big in a few places because she thought it should be. There's a photograph of the courtyard from a window she no longer has access to — the building's windows before the renovation, the linden smaller. Nobody asked her to take it down. She didn't take it down.

There are notes on damp and notes on the specific behavior of old metal-frame windows in winter, and a section that might be a journal and might just be notes, she hasn't decided, written in the first person and not particularly organized. Some of it is very old. Some of it is from last month.

Marek found it by looking for something about a boiler and ended up reading for an hour. He sent her a message saying he'd found it useful. She wrote back. They've met twice in person since.

She doesn't keep it for people to find. She keeps it because it's hers and she's been writing in it for a long time and stopping would feel strange.
