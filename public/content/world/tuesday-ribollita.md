Late March, so the last of the storage onions and whatever the greengrocer had — fennel this week, a little soft at the edges, and a bag of dried beans that had been soaking since morning. Ines had started the pot without a plan. It became something like ribollita except she'd run out of the right bread and used the end of a different loaf, the kind with seeds, which is fine.

The table has four chairs on weekdays and six if you pull the bench from the wall. Tonight there's the bench. Yusuf's dad had stayed to ask about something and then didn't leave, and Daphne had walked in an hour ago and sat with her coffee and her book and was still there, at the corner, turned sideways against the arm of the bench.

Nobody announced that they were staying for dinner. It didn't require announcement.

The windows are fogged from the pot. Ines serves it the way she always does — bowl, spoon, the oil in a small green pitcher for the table. She doesn't ask who wants what. She guesses and is usually right.

Yusuf's dad — Marco, though Ines still sometimes calls him Yusuf's dad by accident — says something about the fennel and she says the seeds help, and then they talk about whether fennel gets better or worse when you cook it down this long, and there isn't a right answer, and they both seem fine with that.

Daphne doesn't say much until the second bowl. Then she says *the bread is different.* Not a complaint. Ines says *I know.* Daphne says *it works.* That's the whole exchange.

The overhead light in the kitchen is warm. There's a reading lamp on in the corner that no one turned on tonight — it's on a timer. The table has a watermark from a mug that's been there for a year. The pot is still on the stove because there's more.

It's a Tuesday. Nobody said it was anything.
