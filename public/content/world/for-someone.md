His friend Selin is getting a new flat. She asked for nothing. He made her something anyway.

He's been thinking about it for two weeks — what she'd actually want, not what would be impressive. She has specific taste: nothing too declarative, nothing that asks you to look at it, something that earns its place by being useful or beautiful in the way you have to be around it for a while to see. He knows this because he knows her.

He made something small. A panel, maybe forty centimeters, the colors pulled from something she'd shown him once — a photograph from a trip she'd taken, the specific quality of that light. He's not copying the photograph, he's taking something from it. The way you take something from a conversation without quoting it.

It took three evenings. Not complicated work — he knows what he's doing — but careful work, the kind where you have to keep the specific person in mind rather than the general idea of what looks good.

He looked at it when it was done. He thought: she'll know where this came from. That's the whole point. Made for one person, legible to that person, not required to be anything for anyone else.

He wrapped it badly because he wraps things badly. She'll unwrap it badly and then hold it for a second and he'll see whether he got it right.

He thinks he got it right.
