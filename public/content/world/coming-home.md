The tram from the station is a specific pleasure after being away. You know the route. You know which stop comes after which stop. You know when to look up for the thing you like to look at — the corner where the canal shows between buildings, the particular rooftop garden that's always doing something seasonal, the section of the 14's route where the buildings give way briefly and you can see further.

The city looks like itself, which after somewhere else looks specific. It's yours not in the sense of ownership but in the sense of accumulated familiarity. You know the scale of it. You know how long it takes to walk from this point to that point. You know the way the light falls in October in this particular neighborhood.

When you open the flat door: that smell, which is the smell of your flat, which you can't smell when you're in it. Three days away is enough. You smell it for the first minute and then you don't, the way you stop smelling the linden, the way you stop noticing the specific weight of your own life when you're in it. But for that minute: yes. This is the place. You live here.

He unpacked. Made coffee. Stood at the window. The courtyard was doing what it does in the early evening in autumn: a few people, the linden turning. Kofi went past with his bike. Lila was out by herself, which meant she was allowed to be out by herself now, which meant something had changed while he was away, the way things change while you're away and you notice by comparison.

He'd been somewhere for a week. The week was good. This was also good, but differently — the good of the familiar, the good of the specific place that is yours.

He finished the coffee. He put the cup in the sink. He was home.
