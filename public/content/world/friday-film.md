The film is already up when Yusuf gets to the courtyard — someone put on the opening titles early, or maybe it's been running for a few minutes, it's hard to tell. He finds a patch of wall to put his back against and sets down the bowl he brought, some of the tomatoes from the roof plus whatever else was in the kitchen, and someone nearby says *oh those look good* without fully looking up from where they're sitting.

The light is that particular summer-evening light where everything is still warm but not hot anymore. The wall behind the screen is a little orange from it.

A folding table by the south entrance has acquired things over the course of the last half hour: a loaf of bread that is about half gone, something in a dish covered with a cloth, a bottle of something, two bottles of something else, a paper bag from the bakery that seems to already be empty, a board with a knife and some cheese and a section of quince paste. Nobody put this together. It assembled.

There are maybe twenty people. Some sitting in chairs that appeared. Some on the stone ledge. A few on a blanket that the Ferreira kids brought down and then spread out and then had their territory gradually expanded by adults who needed somewhere to sit. Marta's daughter is asleep or almost asleep against someone's leg — not her mother's — and nobody is making anything of it.

The film is something from a long time ago. Someone picked it. Nobody argued.

A man Yusuf doesn't know well is explaining something quietly to the person beside him, not about the film, something else, and they both laugh and then quiet down. The courtyard isn't quiet — there's the film, there's murmuring, there's a kid somewhere who is technically asleep but not entirely — but it feels close to quiet. The density of people in a small space all doing something together but not in unison.

Someone brings more bread. The evening gets darker very gradually and then all at once.
