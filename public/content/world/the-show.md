The opener finished twenty minutes ago and Priya still hasn't gotten another drink because she doesn't want to lose her spot near the pillar. The ceiling is low. The room smells like old wood and someone's spilled beer and the candles on the bar ledge that have been burning since before anyone arrived.

The band is three people. The bassist is barefoot. She has been watching the floor since they started and Priya doesn't know if that's focus or shyness or just how she plays. The guitarist said something to the room before the second song that nobody could fully hear and he laughed at himself and started playing anyway.

The drummer keeps almost-smiling at something. Like a private joke that keeps proving itself true.

The song they're in the middle of has a section that keeps almost-resolving and then doesn't. It's been doing this for two minutes. Priya has uncrossed and recrossed her arms without noticing. The woman next to her is nodding but not on the beat — on something else, something looser, the way you nod when you're thinking.

Nobody's recording. Someone has a phone out but it's not raised, it's just there.

The bassist finally looks up. Not at the room — at the guitarist, at something they're passing back and forth between them. She smiles. He doesn't look back but his right shoulder shifts, like he heard it.

The song lands. Not big, not climactic. It just settles.

There's a beat of silence before the clapping. Just long enough to feel like they all held their breath together without planning to.
