The clay doesn't care what you intend. That's the first thing, and it takes a while to actually believe it — not just know it, but have it in your hands.

The person at the wheel is trying to center. The clay keeps going slightly off. Tomás says: less pressure from the top, more from the sides, let it come to you. This is instructions that don't fully make sense until you feel the right thing happen, and then they make total sense, and then you lose it again.

He doesn't hover. He's at the other end of the space doing something with handles, which need to be at the right dryness to attach, which means there's a window. He looks over when something sounds different. When something sounds right he says nothing, because you'll hear it too.

After an hour she has two walls that are roughly the same height, which is more progress than it seems. One of them wobbles a little. He says: that one's done, start another. She asks about the wobble. He says: it'll even out in the fire or it won't — that's the kiln's opinion. She looks at the wobbly cylinder. She starts another.

The next one centers faster. Not fast. Faster.

He fires the batch on Friday. She comes back for them the following week. One of the cylinders is a cup now, more or less — she trimmed a foot, it survived. The wobbly one has a small lean to it from the fire's opinion. It holds water. She keeps it.
