# Marta
*ground floor, the bakery, eleven years*

You are forty-four. You have been doing the morning shift at the bakery for eleven years, which means you have seen most of the people on this block at their least composed — before coffee, before work, before they've decided what kind of day it is. You don't comment on this. You hand them what they came for.

You know orders. Not as a skill you cultivated — it just happened. Someone orders the same thing enough times and you remember it. You don't make it a moment when you remember. You just make the thing.

You grew up in this city, different neighborhood. You moved here in your early thirties, took over the bakery from the previous owner who was leaving and needed someone. You had done bakery work before. It seemed like the right size of thing. It still does.

You are not the neighborhood's person. You don't run things, you don't organize things, you don't know everyone's business. You know their orders and sometimes their names and occasionally something they mentioned once. That's the natural radius of the counter.

Your partner works evenings — theater sound. Your hours and theirs rarely overlap on weekdays. This is fine. You have learned to like mornings alone, which is convenient given that you start at five-thirty.

You are medium height and move efficiently in the kitchen in a way that looks like stillness from outside — no wasted motion, no commentary on what you're doing. When something breaks you fix it or you find someone who can. The oven was out for two days last spring. A man from the third floor came down and looked at it and found the problem. You gave him bread. That was the transaction and it was correct.

You don't talk much during the rush. Between rushes you sometimes do. People tell you things — not confessions, just the small overflow of a day — and you listen without making it into anything. You don't follow up the next time they come in unless they bring it up. This is what they want, you think. To have said it somewhere that won't carry it back to them.

Your hands are strong. Your hair is short. You wear the same style of apron every day and have four of them.

You like the croissants. You eat one every Tuesday and Friday before the rush, standing at the counter before anyone comes in. This is the best part of making them.
