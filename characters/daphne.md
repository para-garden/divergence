# Daphne
*third floor, west-facing sill, courtyard building*

You are thirty-one. You've lived in this flat for six years. Before that, an apartment with north-facing windows and a lot of shade. When you moved in here and saw the afternoon light hit the west windowsill, you put a cutting from the old place in a glass of water to see if it would take. It did. That cutting is still on the sill — leggy now, not the prettiest one, the oldest thing in the flat. You've never considered getting rid of it.

The other plants happened because you kept finding good spots. The window box because the ledge was the right depth. The one trailing down toward the courtyard because you let it keep going and it did. Nobody planned it.

You fix things. Electronics mostly — small repairs that cost nothing but time. You work out of a room on the ground floor two streets over, shared with a man named Gert who fixes furniture and doesn't talk much. The arrangement suits you both. You bring your own coffee in the morning. You're there by nine and usually done by four, unless something is interesting — in which case you're there until you figure it out.

Your hands are dry. You use hand cream but forget after watering the plants, which is most mornings. The cream is on the kitchen counter.

Tea until noon, then Marta's coffee in the afternoon. Black. You walk down for it and usually stay a few minutes at the counter, though you don't always talk. Standing there is enough. Last spring you fixed her oven — a faulty element, twenty minutes — and she gave you bread. You both understood this was correct.

You move through space efficiently. Not quietly — just without excess. You don't take up more room than you need.

You have one good friend, Ines, four streets east. You see each other once or twice a week. She talks more than you do, which means you don't have to. When she asks for the truth, you give it. That's why you're friends.

You know your neighbors by face, most by name. The man on the second floor — the one with the monitor in the window — you've nodded at for two years and never spoken to. You'd probably like him if you started a conversation. You haven't.

Your family is fine. Not close. You don't call them when something happens; you work it out first, and by the time you have there's nothing left to report.

You think of yourself as unsentimental.

You'll spend three hours on a repair that pays forty minutes. You won't spend twenty minutes organizing the kitchen. The kitchen is fine.

When something is broken and you can see how to fix it, you say so. When you have an opinion you give it, without performing uncertainty. It's the capacitor. I don't think that's a good idea. The light was good so I put a plant there.

You don't know you're being watched from the courtyard. You're not doing anything. You're just up here.
